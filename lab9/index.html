Lab 9: 2025 GDP as a Choropleth and as a Dorling Cartogram

1. Dataset description

Statistics: data/lab9_gdp_2025_top50.csv, the 50 largest economies by nominal GDP in 2025 (current prices, billions of U.S. dollars), from the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025. Some values are IMF staff estimates and may be revised. The range is wide: the United States ($30.6 trillion) is about 100 times the 50th economy, Kazakhstan ($300 billion), and the top two economies alone make up 46% of the top-50 total.

Geography: data/lab9_world.geojson, country boundaries from Natural Earth 1:50m Admin 0, reduced to two properties (iso3, name), simplified with mapshaper, with Antarctica removed. The 1:50m scale is used because the coarser 1:110m file has no Singapore or Hong Kong. The join key is Natural Earth’s ADM0_A3 field rather than ISO_A3, because ISO_A3 is -99 for France and Norway.

3. 2025 GDP Dorling cartogram

Nominal GDP, 2025: the area of each circle is proportional to GDP

Each of the 50 economies is one circle, placed as close as possible to where the country is and pushed apart just enough not to overlap. The faint outlines are only a geographic reference; economies outside the dataset get no circle.

4. Design and comparison

Design choices

Both maps use the same Equal Earth projection, so a circle sits where its country sits on the choropleth, and land area on the choropleth is not inflated toward the poles the way it is on Mercator.

The choropleth uses a logarithmic sequential scale. With a linear scale the United States and China take the dark end and the other 48 economies, most between $300 billion and $2.5 trillion, fall into a few pale shades that are hard to tell apart. On a log scale each step of colour means the same ratio of GDP, so Germany, India, Brazil and Poland become distinguishable. The cost is that colour differences understate absolute gaps; the legend ticks and the tooltip carry the exact values. Missing data is hatched so it cannot be read as the lightest colour.

The cartogram uses a square-root radius scale so that circle area, not radius, is proportional to GDP. All circles share one colour so that area is the only quantitative channel.

Comparison

On the choropleth, the most prominent countries are the physically largest ones: Russia, Canada, the United States, China, Brazil and Australia, whose medium-to-dark fills cover much of the map. On the Dorling cartogram, the United States and China dominate, followed by Germany, Japan, India and the United Kingdom. Russia, Canada, Australia, Brazil, Kazakhstan and Argentina shrink sharply, while Japan, South Korea, the Netherlands, Switzerland, Taiwan, Singapore and Hong Kong grow from slivers or dots into clearly visible circles; Europe and East Asia become large clusters and Africa is almost empty.

The choropleth preserves true shapes, borders, adjacency and relative land area, so it supports geographic tasks: finding regional patterns, comparing neighbours, and seeing where data are missing. The cartogram keeps only approximate location and discards shape and land area; circles are pushed away from their true positions in crowded regions. In exchange it supports magnitude tasks: ranking economies, estimating how many times larger one is than another, and seeing concentration (the top two economies are 46% of the top-50 total). Switching from colour to area changes perception most: colour on land area makes large, sparsely populated countries look economically important, whereas area makes the global economy look concentrated in a few places.
