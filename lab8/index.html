Lab 8: Formal Structure vs. Semantic Structure of the DKU Bulletin

1. Bulletin / corpus description

Source: Bulletin of Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction 2021–2022 (400-page PDF, version dated August 2021), official DKU source: V2021-22_DKU_UG_Bulletin.pdf, accessed 25 September 2026. A local copy is kept in data/lab8_dku_ug_bulletin_2021-22.pdf.

The text was extracted with PyMuPDF (lab8/build_corpus.py). The PDF’s 5-level bookmark outline stores the page and the y-position of every heading, so each paragraph is assigned to its chapter (Part), section, subsection and page exactly. One passage is one paragraph or short policy block: bulleted lists are joined to the sentence that introduces them, paragraphs split by a page break are re-joined, and each course-requirement table becomes one passage (“Disciplinary Courses: COMPSCI 201 … (4 credits); …”). Page numbers, headings and the table of contents are dropped. The analysis covers Parts 1–10 up to the start of Course Descriptions (pp. 10–216): the remaining 180 pages are one-paragraph course blurbs, the academic calendar and contacts, which would outnumber every policy passage and turn the topics into academic disciplines.

5. Semantic findings

Each answer can be reproduced in the views above; passage ids (e.g. p0223) can be found by clicking the points mentioned or by searching for a phrase from the quoted text.

  1. What are the major semantic topics?
    KMeans on the embeddings gives ten topics. Four are about the rules of study: Grading, CR/NC & Academic Standing (88 passages), Credit Transfer, Placement & Study Away (77), Leave, Withdrawal & Fees (72) and Academic Integrity & Grievances (28). Two describe the institution and the student experience: Campus Life & DKU Identity (53) and Advising & Academic Support (33). The other four come from the majors chapter: Major Overviews & Tracks (65, descriptive prose) and three kinds of course lists, STEM Course Requirements (69), Humanities & Social Sci. Major Courses (43) and Humanities & Social Sci. Electives (47). On the map the course-list topics form tight, separate islands, because tables of course codes read very differently from prose, while the policy topics sit next to each other in one large continent.
  2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?
    In the chapter matrix, Campus Life & DKU Identity appears in 8 of the 10 Parts, and Credit Transfer, Placement & Study Away and Advising & Academic Support in 7. In the section matrix, Credit Transfer, Placement & Study Away is the most spread-out topic (22 rows): it appears in The Curriculum (Degree Requirements), Academic Procedures (Entrance Credit & Placement, Transfer of Work Elsewhere and nine other sections), Admission (application requirements) and Global Education & Study Away (P8). Rules about how credit is counted are therefore scattered over at least four Parts of the bulletin. Grading, CR/NC & Academic Standing (17 rows) and Leave, Withdrawal & Fees (15 rows in six Parts: fees in P5, deadlines in P4, leave and withdrawal in P6) also cut across the formal structure. The three course-list topics occur only in P10, apart from two STEM passages in Degree Requirements.
  3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?
    Among chapters, P3 The Curriculum has the highest diversity (entropy 2.42 bits over 52 passages). Its passages split between Advising & Support (Signature Work, the Common Core), Credit Transfer (credit totals, language placement), Grading and Major Overviews. P10 Majors (2.21) and P6 Academic Procedures (2.02) follow. P7 Advising & Support is the most uniform (entropy 0: all 10 passages in one topic) and P5 Financial Information is nearly uniform (0.47: 24 of 26 in Leave, Withdrawal & Fees). At section level, Degree Requirements (2.13 over 40 passages) and the Computation and Design major (2.15) are the most mixed, apart from the merged “Other (P6)” row.
  4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?
    Nearest-neighbour search finds several cross-chapter pairs that say nearly the same thing. For example (cosine 0.92), p0151 in Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere (P6), “A Duke Kunshan University student may receive credit for a maximum of 40 credits taken elsewhere…”, and p0340 in Global Education & Study Away (P8), “Students may transfer up to a maximum of 40 credits taken outside of the Duke Kunshan University…”. The grading rule for study abroad in Grading & Grade Requirements (p0223) has its twin in the Study Away section (p0341, 0.94), and the rule for Chinese-as-a-Second-Language placement appears in Degree Requirements (P3), Entrance Credit & Placement (P6) and Requirements for All Majors (P10) (p0047, p0148, p0384). A reader looking up one of these rules in one place would miss the others.
  5. Which passages appear unusual relative to their formal section?
    The detail panel reports each passage’s typicality: its cosine similarity to the average embedding of its section. The least typical passages are the short Signature Work bullet points inside Degree Requirements (p0067 “Two senior capstone…”, 0.33; p0069 “One or more experiential activities after year one”, 0.36). They sit with Advising & Academic Support, not with the credit rules around them. The residence-life room-assignment passage in Student Affairs (p0352, 0.45) is drawn into Leave, Withdrawal & Fees by its deadlines and deposits. The opening essays of US Studies and Cultures and Movements (0.43) are unusual inside their own majors, whose other passages are mostly course tables.
  6. Do “credit”, “graduation”, “registration” and “academic integrity” occur in one semantic region or across several topics?
    credit is everywhere (259 passages). Most of that comes from course tables, where every course ends with “(4 credits)”, but even the 108 prose passages spread over five topics: Grading & Standing (53), Credit Transfer (43), Leave & Fees (6), Advising (4) and Campus Life (2). Credit is a cross-cutting concept rather than a topic of its own. graduation (32 passages, 6 Parts) falls in three neighbouring policy topics: Grading & Standing (14), Credit Transfer (12) and Leave (6). registration is rare (9 passages) and mostly in the Leave & Withdrawal region (5), because registering is described together with return from leave and deadlines. academic integrity is the only term that stays in one place: all 3 passages sit in the Academic Integrity & Grievances cluster.

Design description and findings

Each of the 575 cleaned passages was embedded with the sentence-transformer all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384-d, L2-normalised; lab8/semantic_analysis.py); passages over 180 words were split at sentence boundaries so the model’s 256-token limit never truncates them. UMAP (cosine metric, n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.15, random_state=401) projects the vectors to 2-D for display only. Topics come from KMeans on the original 384-d vectors. Silhouette scores were low for every k (0.09–0.14), so k = 10 was kept for interpretability: k = 6 scored slightly higher but merged academic integrity into grading and leave into fees. Each cluster was labelled by hand from its most central passages and its class-based TF-IDF terms.

On the map, position encodes semantic similarity, hue the topic and area passage length. Ten hues cannot be told apart by colour alone, so topic names are printed at each cluster’s centre and the legend can isolate one topic. The formal section is left to the matrix, whose single-hue shade shows the share of each row’s passages, so small chapters are not washed out by large ones; marginal bars give row size.

Both views share one selection state. Search, filters, matrix cells, row labels and column headers all narrow the same set of passages: the map dims the rest and the matrix recounts. Clicking a passage outlines its cell and its neighbours’ cells, which exposes similar content that lives in a different part of the bulletin.

Findings: formal and semantic structure agree for the majors, whose course tables form separate islands, but not for the rules of study. Credit rules are spread over at least four Parts, the same 40-credit transfer limit is stated in two chapters, and The Curriculum is the most semantically mixed chapter.
