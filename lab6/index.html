Lab 6: GDP Hierarchy Treemaps

A synthetic GDP dataset for 26 countries, nested World → continent → area → country. Each country carries a GDP amount (gdp_billion_usd) and a GDP trend (gdp_status: Increase, Unchanged, Decrease). The flat CSV is converted to nested JSON in convert_hierarchy.py, then rendered below as two treemaps that use different tiling algorithms.

Each treemap opens on the six continents, sized by total GDP. Click a continent to zoom into its areas and countries; use the “All continents” link above the chart to zoom back out. Once zoomed in, hover a country rectangle for its exact GDP and status — thin borders mark the areas within that continent.

Design description

GDP is a ratio quantity, so it drives rectangle area — the channel treemaps are built for, letting relative economic size be read at a glance without a legend. GDP status is a three-level nominal category, so it takes rectangle colour (green/grey/red for Increase/Unchanged/ Decrease), a channel independent of area so size and trend can be read separately. Showing all four levels (world/continent/area/ country) on screen at once badly squeezed small continents such as Africa and Oceania, whose countries became slivers too thin to label. Instead each treemap opens on just the six continents, coloured by continent identity and sized by total GDP; clicking one zooms that continent to fill the whole canvas, revealing its area bands and country rectangles with room to read. Squarify (Treemap A) keeps rectangles close to square, which makes individual countries easy to compare and click, but it reorders items freely to hit that aspect ratio, so the reading order within a group feels arbitrary. Binary (Treemap B) recursively splits each group in two, producing narrower, more elongated rectangles and a more strictly ordered layout by descending GDP, at some cost to the squareness of individual cells.
