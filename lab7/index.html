Lab 7: Temporal Commercial Network

Sixty days of commercial transactions among 12 companies (lab7_assignment_transactions_60days.csv), drawn as a force-directed network that changes over time. Each link is an undirected commercial relationship between two companies; a link is active when the pair traded during the current time window.

Press Play to animate day by day, or drag the slider to inspect any day by hand. Hover a company or a link for its exact numbers, and drag companies to rearrange them. Newly appearing links fade in extra thick and shrink to their real width; relationships that stop trading fade out, and companies with no trades in the window are drawn faded. The “Link window” controls how many recent days count as active (1 day is very sparse; 7 days is the default).

Design description

The layout uses d3.forceSimulation() with a link force, many-body repulsion, weak centering and collision avoidance. Every frame recomputes the active links from the transactions inside the time window, aggregates repeated trades between the same pair into one link, and lets the simulation re-settle briefly. Node size (a ratio quantity) shows how much a company is trading right now, and the scale is fixed across all 60 days so a node that grows really is trading more. Sector is a nominal category, so it takes colour; region is a second nominal category, so it takes shape, a channel that stays legible next to colour. Link width shows the amount traded, link colour shows the dominant transaction type (a nominal category, so hue), and dashing marks links that connect companies in different regions. The transaction count is in the link tooltip.

To preserve the mental map, the same 12 node objects are kept for the whole animation (inactive companies fade rather than disappearing), the layout is pre-settled on the full 60-day network before playback starts, every node is gently pulled back towards that settled “home” position, and each update only re-heats the simulation (alpha) instead of restarting it. Playback can be paused at any time and the slider gives frame-by-frame inspection.

Temporal-network questions

Observations below use the default 7-day window; drag the slider to the days mentioned to see them in the network.

  1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?
    Connectivity builds up and then steps up in two stages. On day 1 only 3 links are active (6 of the 12 companies); by day 2 every company has traded, and by day 5 the network is a single connected component. Days 5–20 hold steady at 15 links; days 21–26 rise to 17–19 links, days 27–40 fall back to 15, and days 42–45 jump to 24 links before settling at 18 for days 47–60. The value traded per week grows much more steadily than the link count: the “Total transaction value” box goes from about $0.5–0.6M (days 7–20) to about $0.65–0.7M (days 27–40) and $1.0M (days 47–60), roughly 1.8 times as much by the end, carried over thicker links.
  2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?
    Fusion Electronics is the largest node over days 1–20 (about $372K in total) and dominates days 21–40 with about $1.05M, more than twice the next company, because it adds links to Harbor Shipping, Lumen Distribution and Juniper Stores. Over days 41–60 the lead passes to Ion Systems (about $1.10M, up from $131K) and Cedar Retail (about $0.90M, up from $133K), which each gain three new partners; Fusion Electronics falls back to about $0.56M. Apex Textiles also more than doubles in the last third (about $214K → $476K). Evergreen Foods is the least active company throughout: it has a single partner (Cedar Retail) until day 42, when it also starts trading with Lumen Distribution.
  3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?
    Yes, mainly in the first 20 days. On day 1 the network is three separate pairs (Cedar–Evergreen, Granite–Harbor, Ion–Lumen) with six companies idle. On days 3–4 it forms two clusters of six: Apex, BlueRiver, Cedar, Delta, Evergreen and Fusion versus Granite, Harbor, Ion, Juniper, Keystone and Lumen. The Fusion–Granite link first appears on day 5 and joins them; with the default 7-day window the network is a single component on every day from then on. Switching the window to 3 days shows that this bridge is thin: the two halves separate again on days 8–9, 13–14 and 18–19, and never after day 21, once Fusion’s new links to the other half keep them connected. Later structure is hub-and-spoke rather than clustered: Fusion in days 21–40, then Ion, Cedar and Fusion in days 41–60.
  4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?
    A stable core of 10 relationships trades from the first days to the last (for example Cedar–Evergreen, Ion–Lumen, Apex–Fusion, Apex–BlueRiver, Delta–Fusion). Three links disappear after day 20: BlueRiver–Delta, Harbor–Keystone and Cedar–Fusion. Days 21–24 bring Fusion’s new links to Harbor, Lumen and Juniper plus Delta–Keystone, and Fusion–Granite becomes much stronger (about $33K in days 5–20 to $250K in days 21–40). Those five links and Granite–Harbor all end by day 40. Days 41–42 add eight new links, six of them around Ion Systems and Cedar Retail (e.g. Cedar–Keystone, Cedar–Granite, Fusion–Ion). Ion–Juniper goes quiet for days 21–40 and returns much stronger (about $127K → $202K per 20 days), and Delta–Fusion also strengthens (about $126K → $154–160K).
  5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?
    More cross-regional, especially in the last third. In a 7-day window 60% of active links connect different regions on days 5–20 and 27–40 (with a brief rise to 65–68% on days 21–26), then 71% on days 42–45 and 67% on days 47–60 (see the “Links that cross regions” box and the dashed links). The rise comes from new relationships: all four links added on days 21–24 and 7 of the 8 added on days 41–42 connect different regions (the exception is Evergreen–Lumen, both in North America). The stable core is split evenly (5 of its 10 links cross regions), so the network becomes more globally integrated mainly through its newer, stronger relationships.
