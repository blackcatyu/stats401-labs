Lab 2: Multivariate Visualization with D3

This visualization explores the relationship among study hours, exam score, major, and year level.

Assignment: City Data — A Compass-Layout Multivariate View

Regions are arranged by their real-world direction (North top, South bottom, West left, East right). Within each panel, development level sets horizontal position, population sets bubble size, and temperature sets color.

Design Justification

I mapped each variable to a visual channel that matches its measurement type. Average temperature (interval, continuous) is placed on the x-axis, since position is the most precise channel and lets viewers compare exact values. Development level (ordinal) is placed on the y-axis in three stacked bands, so its natural order (Low, Medium, High) reads directly as vertical height, with higher development sitting higher up. Population (ratio) is encoded as bubble size; I used a square-root scale so that circle area, not radius, is proportional to population, avoiding visual exaggeration of large cities. Region (nominal) is encoded with color, because color distinguishes unordered categories without implying any ranking. Together, these choices let viewers quickly spot patterns — for example, whether warmer cities tend to have lower development levels, or whether larger cities cluster in particular regions.
