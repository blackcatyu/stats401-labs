Lab 4: Cleaning Web Data for Visualization

14,640 tweets directed at six US airlines (Feb 2015) were cleaned and deduplicated to 14,485 records, then every one was scored for sentiment with a Twitter-tuned RoBERTa model (cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest). The chart below shows the resulting sentiment mix for each airline, ordered from most negative to most positive average score.

About this analysis

The raw dataset is a 2015 Crowdflower scrape of tweets mentioning six US airlines, redistributed on GitHub as Tweets.csv. Cleaning dropped two columns that were over 99% empty, collapsed 80 exact-duplicate rows plus 75 duplicate tweet_ids (14,640 → 14,485 rows), and parsed tweet_created into date, hour, and weekday. Sentiment came from a RoBERTa model fine-tuned on Twitter text, which outputs negative/neutral/positive probabilities for every one of the 14,485 cleaned tweets; a continuous score (positive − negative probability) is averaged per airline for this chart. RoBERTa's output is a model estimate, not ground truth — it can miss sarcasm and airline-specific slang, and the dataset also keeps human-labeled sentiment for comparison. The chart encodes sentiment (negative/neutral/positive share, stacked) against airline, and shows two clear patterns: US Airways and American have the largest negative share (65% and 59%) and most negative average score (−0.44 and −0.37), while Delta and Virgin America are the only airlines with a positive average score and the largest positive share (34% and 38%).
